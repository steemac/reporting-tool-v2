What this tool is

QuickFile Reports is a companion tool for your QuickFile account. You give it a backup zip file exported from QuickFile, and it builds 58 reports from it: the standard statements, ledgers, sales and purchase reports you know from QuickFile, plus an Advanced section of reports QuickFile doesn't offer — like payment allocations, profit by property, year-on-year comparisons, bank exports across every account, address lists and data checks.

It runs entirely in your web browser: there's nothing to install and no account or sign-up. It works alongside your QuickFile account rather than replacing it — QuickFile is still where you raise invoices, record payments and file your VAT return. It isn't an official QuickFile feature.

Where your numbers come from

Everything you see comes from the single backup zip file you choose. That backup contains a full copy of your invoices, purchases, payments, nominal ledger, contacts, bank statement lines and team logins, as a set of files QuickFile writes out for you.

When you load the zip, the tool reads those files and matches them up the way an accountant would: each invoice line is joined to its client or supplier, the nominal code it was posted to and its project tag; each payment is linked to the invoice it was allocated to; and the financial statements are added up from every posting in your nominal ledger. The bank reports use your bank statement lines directly.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. The zip file is opened and read on your own computer, inside this browser tab — it's never sent to QuickFile, to us, or to any other server. If you close the tab or refresh the page, the data is gone and you'd need to choose the zip again.

Using the tool, step by step

  1. Export a backup from QuickFile. In QuickFile, go to Account Settings → All Settings → Monthly Backups, enter an email address, and click Run Now. You'll receive an email shortly afterwards with a link to download your backup — save the zip file somewhere you can find it (you don't need to unzip it).
    The Monthly backups option under Account Settings → All Settings.
    The Backup Settings page — enter your email under "Send to" and click "Run now".
  2. Choose the zip file. Click Choose backup .zip at the top of the page and select the file you downloaded. A short progress bar shows while your backup is read — it takes about five seconds — and then the page switches from the example figures to your own data. The status next to the search box confirms your backup is loaded and the latest date it contains.
  3. Pick a report. Browse the report cards, use the report library on the left, or type in the Find a report box (for example "trial balance", "statement" or "untagged"). Each card tells you where the same report lives in QuickFile, or that it's only available here.
  4. Narrow it down. Each report has its own filters — dates, a client or supplier, a nominal code, a bank account, a status or a search box, depending on the report. The table updates as soon as you change anything, and the figures in the blue summary strip update with it. Reset filters puts everything back to the defaults.
  5. Sort and review. On list reports, click any column heading to sort by it; click again to reverse the order. Statements such as the Profit and loss keep their accounting order instead.
  6. Export what you're looking at. Export CSV saves the table on screen — filtered and sorted — as a .csv file you can open in Excel or Google Sheets. Copy CSV puts the same data on your clipboard to paste straight into a spreadsheet.

The tool opens with example figures already in place, so you can try every report before loading a real backup.

Reporting on project tags? Sales by project, Profit by project, and anything else grouped by property or job need project data to be in the backup. When you create the backup, tick the Include Projects box — it isn't ticked by default, and without it your project tags won't come through.

Finding your way around

Reports are grouped under six headings. The first five match the areas of QuickFile they come from; the sixth, Advanced, holds the extra reports and is split into its own areas (sales analysis, purchase analysis, customer and supplier insights, payment allocations, projects, banking, contacts and addresses, team, and data checks).

  • Heading buttons under the search box jump straight to a heading. Inside Advanced, a second row of buttons jumps to each area.
  • The report library down the left-hand side lists every report and highlights the one you have open. (On a phone it's replaced by the report cards.)
  • All reports above a report's title takes you back to the full list.
  • Links between reports: in the client and supplier lists, rankings and monthly balance reports, clicking a name opens that contact's statement.
  • Greyed-out reports need a file your backup doesn't include — for example team reports need the team members file, and bank reports need bank statement files.

Financial statements

Profit and loss, balance sheet and trial balance, straight from your nominal ledger.

All five statements are worked out from every posting in your nominal ledger, using QuickFile's own code ranges: 0001–3999 are balance sheet accounts and 4000–9999 are profit and loss accounts. Opening balances are already part of the ledger, so nothing needs to be added by hand. The Balance sheet shows a Difference figure, which should always be £0.00, and the Trial balance's closing debit and credit columns should always agree — both are quick confirmation that the backup was read completely.

Profit and loss

Income, cost of sales and expenses for the dates you pick, grouped by nominal code and worked out from every posting in the nominal ledger (accruals basis — the same basis as QuickFile's own P&L).

In QuickFile: Reports → Profit and loss

What the business owns and owes at the date you pick, from every nominal ledger posting up to and including that date. Profit and loss to date is shown under capital and reserves.

In QuickFile: Reports → Balance sheet

Trial balance

In QuickFile: Reports → Trial Balance

Income and expenditure breakdown

Advanced

Extra reports QuickFile doesn't offer, or only offers one account or screen at a time — sales and purchase analysis, customer insights, projects, banking exports, contacts, team and data checks.

These are the reports that go beyond QuickFile's own screens: combinations it doesn't offer, lists it only shows one account or record at a time, and checks that help you tidy your books. Each one says whether there's a closest equivalent in QuickFile or whether it only exists here.

Sales analysis

Line-level sales and trends by client, item and nominal code.

The monthly summaries show the 12 months ending a month you pick, with every client, item or code that appears anywhere in your data listed even if it had no activity in that window. The year-on-year reports put five years side by side, either as calendar years or as 12-month periods ending on a date you choose. Where item names weren't used on invoices, the line description is used instead, so similar lines typed differently appear separately.

Customer and supplier insights

Who matters most, who has gone quiet and who pays slowly.

Top clients

Clients ranked by net sales for the dates you pick, with each client's share of the total, average invoice, first and most recent invoice, months since their last invoice and what they still owe. Useful for spotting reliance on a few clients or clients who have gone quiet.

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

Top suppliers

Suppliers ranked by net spend for the dates you pick, with each supplier's share, average purchase, first and most recent purchase and anything still unpaid.

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

How quickly clients pay

For every fully paid sales invoice, the days from issue to the final payment, averaged per client, with the slowest payment and the share paid within the client's payment terms (terms of 0 days mean due on receipt).

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

Payment allocations

Which payments and credits were allocated to which invoice, with prepayments and split payments marked.

How to read the markers: each invoice gets its own colour, and every payment allocated to it carries the same colour with an arrow to the invoice number. The bank icon means the payment matches a line on that bank account's statement on the same day. The split icon means one bank payment was shared across several invoices — the tool only marks this when the individual amounts add up to a single bank line. A payment dated before its invoice is shown as paid before invoice, and the invoice line then says how much was already prepaid. In the Timeline layout a negative running balance is shown as a credit.

Client payment allocations

For one client, every invoice and every payment in date order, showing which invoice each payment was allocated to, payments made before the invoice (prepayments), payments split across several invoices, and the running balance. Switch to By invoice to see each invoice followed by the payments against it.

Closest in QuickFile: Each invoice's payment history, one invoice at a time

Supplier payment allocations

For one supplier, every purchase invoice and payment in date order, showing which purchase each payment was allocated to, payments made before the invoice, split payments and the running balance owed. Switch to By invoice to see each purchase followed by its payments.

Profit by project

Net rent income minus net costs (repairs, utilities, etc.) tagged to the same property, by month, for the 12 months ending the month you pick. Profit = sales net minus purchases net sharing that project tag.

Banking

Statement exports, untagged items and balance checks across every account.

Bank reports use only the bank statement lines in your backup — never figures from invoices. Each account is labelled with its nominal code and name where the tool can identify it with confidence from your nominal ledger; otherwise it's shown as "Bank account" plus its number. Only accounts with an opening balance recorded in QuickFile start from that figure; the rest start from zero.

Bank account summary

One row per bank account in the backup: its nominal code, the first and last transaction dates, the number of transactions, total money in and out, and how many are still untagged. The closing balance includes an opening balance where QuickFile has one.

Closest in QuickFile: Banking → All bank accounts

Closest in QuickFile: Banking → a bank statement → Export to CSV (one account at a time)

Closest in QuickFile: Banking → a bank statement (untagged lines)

Money in and out by month

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

Bank account balances

Opening balance (where known) and running closing balance for each bank account found in the backup's Bank_Accounts folder, at monthly, half-monthly (the 15th and last day of each month) or yearly checkpoints ending the date you pick — so you can check whether the balance at a point in time matches the real statement.

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

Closing balance at the end of every day in one calendar month, for each bank account. Pick any date and the whole month it falls in is shown.

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

Address lines are in separate columns so the export drops straight into a mail merge or label template, with a one-line version at the end for pasting into letters.

Team

Who has access to the account and when they logged in.

Team members

Each user with access to this QuickFile account: name, email, telephone, whether they're an administrator, when they were added, their last login and how many logins are in the backup's login history.

Closest in QuickFile: Account Settings → Team management

Team login history

The login history included in the backup: the date and time, the team member, their IP address and the browser and device they used.

Data checks

Spot possible duplicates and missing or deleted document numbers.

These reports point you to things worth a second look — they don't change anything. A possible duplicate may simply be a regular bill of the same amount, and a gap in numbering usually means a record was deleted in QuickFile (the report says when the backup shows it as deleted).

Possible duplicate purchases

Purchase invoices that share a supplier and gross amount with another purchase dated within the number of days you pick — worth checking in case the same bill was entered twice. Regular payments of the same amount (rent, subscriptions) will show up if you widen the window.

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

Gaps in numbering

Checks each numbering sequence (sales invoices, credit notes and purchase receipt numbers) for numbers that are missing between the first and last one in the backup. A gap usually means a record was deleted.

Advanced — not available in QuickFile

Exporting your results

Every report has the same two buttons. Export CSV saves a .csv file named after the report and today's date (for example quickfile-trial-balance-2026-09-27.csv). Copy CSV copies the same data to your clipboard. Both include exactly what's on screen — your filters and sort order — plus any section headings and total rows.

On very long reports, such as the bank transactions export or nominal ledger detail, the screen shows the first 1,500 rows to keep the page quick, but Export CSV and Copy CSV always include every row.

Good to know

  • Your backup is a snapshot. QuickFile backups are taken from a copy of your data that can be up to 24 hours old, so something you entered very recently may not appear yet.
  • Figures can differ slightly from QuickFile's own screens where definitions differ (for example net versus gross, or which date a line counts against). QuickFile remains the source of truth for your accounts.
  • Some QuickFile reports aren't included — cash-based profit and loss, debtor and creditor ageing, cash-basis project figures, VAT returns and the statement of cash flows — because the backup doesn't contain everything needed to reproduce them exactly. Use QuickFile for those.
  • Nothing is saved between visits. Reload your backup each time you open the tool.
