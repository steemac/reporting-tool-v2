Getting started
How do I get a backup from QuickFile?

In QuickFile, go to Account Settings → All Settings → Monthly Backups, enter an email address and click Run Now. You'll receive an email with a link to download your backup — that's the zip file to choose in this tool. You don't need to unzip it.

If you use project tags, tick Include Projects before you run the backup (see below).

Do I need a QuickFile account to use this?

You need a QuickFile backup zip, which means access to a QuickFile account to create one. The tool itself doesn't ask you to sign in or create an account.

Why does it take a few seconds to load my backup?

After you choose your zip, a progress bar shows for about five seconds while the tool opens the backup, reads your invoices and purchases, matches payments and bank lines, and builds the reports. Very large backups can take a little longer — the bar waits until everything is ready before showing your data.

What browsers or devices does this work on?

Any modern browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. It also works on phones and tablets; on a small screen the report library is replaced by the report cards, and wide tables scroll sideways. The reports are easiest to read on a larger screen.

Is this an official QuickFile product?

No — this is an independent companion tool built to complement QuickFile, not an official QuickFile feature. Your account and data stay in QuickFile; this tool only reads a backup you choose to export.

Your data and privacy
Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The backup zip is read entirely inside your browser tab. It's never sent to QuickFile, to us or to any other server — closing the tab clears everything.

Will my report choice and filters be remembered next time?

No — nothing is saved between visits, so you'll need to choose your backup again each time. You can bookmark a particular report (its web address ends in something like #r=trial_balance), and the tool will open on that report, but you'll still need to load your backup.

What if I close the browser tab or refresh the page?

Your loaded data is cleared and the tool goes back to the example figures. Choose your backup zip again to carry on.

Finding and using reports
How are the reports organised?

Under six headings: Financial statements, Nominal ledger, Sales and clients, Purchases and suppliers, Products and Advanced. The first five hold the standard reports you'll recognise from QuickFile, and each report card shows where the same report lives in QuickFile. Use the search box, the heading buttons or the report library on the left to find what you need.

What's in the Advanced section?

Reports QuickFile doesn't offer, or only offers one record or account at a time. They're grouped into areas:

  • Sales analysis and purchase analysis (line-item detail, monthly summaries, year-on-year)
  • Customer and supplier insights (top clients and suppliers, how quickly clients pay)
  • Payment allocations (which payment paid which invoice)
  • Projects (project summary, sales and profit by project)
  • Banking (all-account exports, untagged lines, balance checks)
  • Contacts and addresses, Team, and Data checks
Why is a report greyed out?

It needs a file your backup doesn't contain. For example, the team reports need the team members file and the bank reports need the bank statement files. If you think the file should be there, try exporting a fresh backup.

Can I download the results as a file?

Yes. Export CSV saves the table on screen — with your filters and sort order — as a .csv file you can open in Excel or Google Sheets. Copy CSV copies the same data to your clipboard instead. On very long reports only the first 1,500 rows are shown on screen, but both buttons always include every row.

Can I jump from a list to a client's or supplier's statement?

Yes — in the client and supplier lists, the monthly balance reports and the top clients and suppliers rankings, click a name to open that contact's statement.

Understanding the figures
Why don't the figures here exactly match a report inside QuickFile?

This tool calculates everything itself from the backup's data rather than using QuickFile's report engine, so definitions can differ slightly — for example net versus gross, or which date a line is counted against. QuickFile remains the source of truth for your accounts.

Also remember that QuickFile backups are taken from a copy of your data that can be up to 24 hours old, so very recent entries may not appear yet.

Why aren't VAT returns, ageing or cash-based reports included?

The backup doesn't hold everything needed to reproduce those reports exactly — for example due dates for ageing, or QuickFile's VAT scheme settings and adjustments. Rather than show figures that might not match, those reports are left out: please use QuickFile for cash-based profit and loss, debtor and creditor ageing, VAT returns and the statement of cash flows.

How do I check the statements have been read correctly?

Two quick checks: the Balance sheet's Difference figure should be £0.00, and the Trial balance's closing debit and credit columns should agree. If both are true, every posting in your nominal ledger has been read.

What do the colours and icons on the payment allocations report mean?

Each invoice gets its own colour, and the payment allocated to it shows the same colour with an arrow to the invoice number. The bank icon means the payment matches a line on your bank statement on the same day. The split icon means one bank payment was shared across several invoices. Paid before invoice marks a prepayment, and a negative running balance is shown as a credit.

Why don't I see my project or property tags?

Reports grouped by project need project data in the backup. When you create the backup in QuickFile, tick Include Projects — it isn't ticked by default, and without it your tags won't come through.

Why does a bank account show no nominal code or name?

The tool only labels an account when it can match it to a nominal code with confidence from your nominal ledger. If it can't, it still shows the account and all its transactions — it just leaves the code and name blank rather than guessing.

Why are some addresses, emails or bank details blank?

The contact reports show exactly what's saved on each client and supplier record in QuickFile. If a field is blank here, it's blank in QuickFile too — add it there and it will appear in your next backup.

Does a possible duplicate or a gap in numbering mean something is wrong?

Not necessarily. A possible duplicate can be a regular bill of the same amount, and a gap in numbering usually means a record was deleted in QuickFile. These reports simply point out things worth a second look.

A report looks wrong — what should I check first?

Check you've loaded your own backup rather than the example data (the status next to the search box confirms it), then check the report's dates and filters — a figure that looks missing is often just outside the range selected. If something you entered recently is missing, export a fresh backup a little later.

How large a backup can this handle?

There's no hard limit — backups with tens of thousands of ledger postings and bank lines work fine. A very large backup covering many years may just take a few extra seconds to load.

About QuickFile
Does this replace QuickFile?

No. You still raise invoices, record payments, reconcile your bank and file returns in QuickFile as normal. This tool only reads a backup of that data to give you extra reports and exports.
